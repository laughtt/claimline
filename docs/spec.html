AWS CDS Agentic AI Partner Hackathon · submit Tue Oct 27, 2026

Claimline

One messaging core for insurers, two products. BothSides turns a car crash into a neutral joint report that both drivers approve. First Call reaches every policyholder in a disaster zone, opens their claims and tells adjusters who to help first. Everything runs over WhatsApp, RCS, SMS and email, with no app to install.

Revised Mon Oct 19, 2026: plan weekdays corrected to the 2026 calendar; the photo-and-video weekend is now only Sun Oct 25.

1 · Overview

Module A · car claims

BothSides

Both drivers scan one QR code and tell their side in their own language: text, voice notes, photos, licence and policy. The agent reconciles the two versions into agreed facts, facts only one driver gave, and conflicts. It asks follow-up questions only about the conflicts. Both drivers approve the draft, and the same file goes to both insurers.

Target prize: Best WhatsApp ($10K), then 1st place.

Module B · natural catastrophes

First Call

A hurricane, earthquake, flood or wildfire alert hits a region. The insurer messages every opted-in policyholder inside the hazard zone with SAFE / DAMAGE / NEED HELP. The agent reads the replies, opens a first notice of loss, triages photo damage and chases people who don't answer. Adjusters get a ranked worklist by email.

Target prize: 1st, 2nd or 3rd place (RCS-first).

3 · BothSides: the car-crash flow

  1. Driver A reports. Driver A messages the insurer's WhatsApp number, or scans the QR code on the policy card. The agent asks about injuries first. If anyone is hurt, it shows the emergency number and pauses the intake. Next it gets the location (a WhatsApp location pin), the time and photos.
  2. Driver B joins. The agent shows driver A a QR code and a link (wa.me/<number>?text=JOIN%20<caseCode>) to show the other driver. Driver B scans it and taps send. Because driver B started the conversation, it counts as user-initiated: no template is needed and no phone numbers are swapped. Driver B doesn't need to be a customer. If driver B has no WhatsApp, a short link starts the same intake over RCS, falling back to SMS.
  3. Each side tells their story. Each driver uses their own language: text, voice notes, photos of damage and the scene, their licence and their policy card. The agent extracts structured facts from each piece of evidence and links every fact to the evidence it came from.
  4. Reconcile. The agent compares the two fact sets and sorts each fact as AGREED, ONLY A / ONLY B, or CONFLICT. It asks each driver about each conflict once, in neutral wording ("Driver B remembers the light as red. What do you remember?"). It never assigns fault.
  5. Both approve. Each driver gets the draft in their own language in a WhatsApp Flow: review the facts, add a comment, approve. A driver can add their own version, but cannot change the other driver's statements. Remaining conflicts stay marked as disputed.
  6. File to both insurers. SES sends the joint report (PDF + JSON + photos) to both insurers' claims inboxes. Each driver gets a copy. When an adjuster replies by email, SES inbound routes the reply back to that adjuster's own customer on WhatsApp.
Why driver B takes part. The report is neutral, like the European Accident Statement: it belongs to both drivers, both insurers get the same file, and each driver approves it before it is sent. Driver B gets the other driver's verified details and their own copy, and avoids weeks of phone tag. This is the first question an insurance judge will ask, so the answer goes on its own slide.

4 · First Call: the natural-catastrophe flow

  1. Trigger. A poller reads official feeds every 5 minutes: NWS CAP alerts (US), the NHC hurricane forecast cone, USGS earthquakes M≥6 with ShakeMap, and GDACS (global, including Mexico). An operator can also draw a zone on the console by hand. Every event becomes a hazard polygon.
  2. Find the people. A point-in-polygon check over the insured risk addresses produces the outreach list. It is ordered by risk: vulnerable flag, then distance to the event centre, then type of policy.
  3. Reach them. In RCS markets, a rich card with SAFE / DAMAGE / NEED HELP buttons, falling back to SMS. Where WhatsApp is primary (Mexico, LatAm), an approved WhatsApp template with the same three quick replies. Messages go out at a controlled rate that stays within each channel's throughput limit.
  4. Understand the replies. The agent turns free text and voice notes into structured needs. "Safe, but the roof is gone and the kids are with me" becomes safe=true, damage=roof, dependants=2, shelter_need=possible. DAMAGE opens a first notice of loss on the spot and asks for 3 photos. Bedrock vision triages severity from 1 to 4 and checks the photo's EXIF time and place.
  5. Chase silence. No reply in 30 minutes → the other channel. After 2 hours → email. After 6 hours → the policyholder goes on a named agent's call list. The ladder stops as soon as anyone replies on any channel.
  6. Adjuster worklist. Every 15 minutes, SES sends each adjuster team a ranked batch: NEED HELP first, then severity 4, then everyone else. When an adjuster replies "ASSIGN 12 34" or "CALLED 12", the case updates and the policyholder is told what happens next.

9 · Agent design

Tools (Strands @tool)

ToolPurpose
get_case / update_caseRead and write case state and approvals.
lookup_policyFind a policy by number, plate or phone. Returns status and covers.
extract_factsTurns one message or piece of evidence into typed facts (schema below).
reconcilePlain code, not the LLM: compares the A and B fact sets field by field. The LLM only judges whether two free-text values mean the same thing.
triage_photoBedrock vision: damage type, severity 1–4, EXIF check, perceptual hash.
transcribe_voiceTranscribes a voice note from S3 and returns the text and detected language.
sendOne outbound message. The Send Lambda chooses the channel. The text passes Guardrails first.
render_reportBuilds the joint report or FNOL as HTML, converts it to PDF, and stores it in S3.
handoff_humanInjury, NEED HELP, abuse or a confused user → stop and give a named person and phone number.

Fact schema

{
  "field": "signal_state | speed_kmh | direction | lane | impact_point | time | location | injuries | damage_part | third_party_plate | weather",
  "value": "red",
  "party": "A",
  "confidence": 0.82,
  "evidence": [{"type": "voice", "s3": "media/c123/a/v1.ogg", "quote": "the light had just turned red"}],
  "language": "es"
}

Rules the agent never breaks

  • It says it is an AI in its first message.
  • It asks about injuries before anything else. If anyone is hurt, it gives the emergency number and stops the intake.
  • It never says who is at fault and never says whether a claim is covered.
  • It never shows one driver's personal data to the other driver. Each sees only the shared crash facts and the other driver's insurer.
  • It hands off to a named human whenever someone needs help, is upset, or asks for a person.

Numbers to measure (none invented)

  • BothSides: 10 public dashcam crash videos as ground truth. Two volunteers narrate each one from opposite sides, with a deliberate bias, in Spanish and English. We report the % of real conflicts the agent caught, false conflicts it raised, and median minutes to a report both drivers approved.

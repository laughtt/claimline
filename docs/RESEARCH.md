## Studies behind the design

| Study | Finding | What Claimline does |
|---|---|---|
| [Loftus & Palmer (1974)](https://www.simplypsychology.org/?p=11466) | "Smashed" gave a 40.8 mph speed estimate and "contacted" 31.8 mph; "smashed" made 32% recall broken glass that wasn't there, against 14% for "hit" | Neutral questions; the agent never suggests a speed, cause or fault |
| Gabbert, Memon & Allan (2003); [Gabbert, Memon & Wright (2006)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03194137) | Witnesses who discuss an event pick up details they never saw (about 71% against 0% without discussion, quoted second-hand) | Each person reports in their own chat and never sees the other's answers |
| [Xiao et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10700v2), ACM TOCHI 27(3) | About 600 participants and 5,200+ answers: a chatbot got more informative, relevant, specific and clear answers than a web survey | WhatsApp, one question at a time, the person's own words saved as evidence |
| Nielsen & Landauer (1993), INTERCHI '93 | Five test users find about 85% of usability problems | The next step: a five-person test (see the README's Status section) |

## Limits

- The US and global studies measure markets unlike Peru's. What transfers is the mechanism (communication and speed keep customers), not the numbers.
- Claimline handles the intake of a claim (first notice of loss). It doesn't check coverage, estimate damage, detect fraud or pay.
- None of these figures were measured with Claimline users. Our own numbers are in the README.

One number, two paths

Every claim has one of two shapes.

After asking whether anyone is hurt, Claimline sends two WhatsApp buttons. The choice is saved in code, so no step gets skipped.

Just me

A loss of your own, reported from where it happened.

A location pin, when it happened, how high the water got, what was damaged and photos. One approval closes the case, and the PDF arrives in the chat.

With someone else

Two drivers, two phones, one fair report.

Each reports in their own chat and language. Plain code compares the versions; the agent asks only about what doesn't match.

The AI never decides fault.

Questions stay neutral, and disputes stay in the report in each person's own words. Approving means "this is what I said", not "I accept the other version".

Rule from Loftus and Palmer (1974): one loaded word changes what witnesses remember.

From a voice note to an adjuster's map in minutes.

Text, voice notes, photos and a pin, at any hour, in Spanish or English. No app to install, no call centre.

Safety first

The first question is whether anyone is hurt. If someone is, the agent gives the emergency number and hands the case to a person.

Report it your way

Voice notes go through Amazon Transcribe. Plates are asked for in writing, because speech recognition can mishear them.

Every fact with its evidence

A Strands agent on Bedrock AgentCore asks one question at a time and saves each fact with the person's own words.

Compare in code

With two people, each fact is AGREED, ONLY_A, ONLY_B or CONFLICT. Claude only judges whether two free-text values mean the same thing.

Approve in a WhatsApp Flow

Each person reviews the draft in the chat. Two rejections without a correction hand the case to a person.

PDF and claims board

The report is built in code as a PDF, stored in S3 and sent in the chat. The insurer sees the case on its map.

When the rain hits Piura, adjusters see where.

Every case lands on a live map with its status, conflicts, the whole conversation, each fact with its evidence, and the PDF.

One WhatsApp number for every line of insurance.

Claimline is the intake: it hands each insurer a complete, evidence-backed case, and deploys into the insurer's own AWS account.

Catastrophe surge

One number for everyone. Serverless, so it scales when thousands report at once, with cases clustered on a map.

Home and property

Floods, huaicos, storms and fire, with a pin, the water level, damaged items and photos.

Agriculture

Peru's catastrophic crop insurance (SAC) pays S/800 per hectare. Field reports with a pin and photos fit the "Just me" path.

Auto and SOAT

A neutral joint report from both drivers, plates typed and checked, conflicts flagged, a PDF for each.

SMEs and shops

Owners report stock and equipment losses by voice, without stopping work.

Microinsurance and brokers

A low-cost channel for low-premium policies, in the chat people already use.

Claimline doesn't check coverage, estimate damage, score fraud or pay; it feeds those steps. SAC figures: Midagri via El Peruano and Agraria.pe.

Built on AWS

Serverless, pay per use, one command to deploy.

With no traffic it costs almost nothing. In a flood it scales with the claims. The whole stack is AWS CDK.
